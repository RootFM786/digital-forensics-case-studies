# Case Study 1 — First Response and Evidence Preservation

## Scenario

The original university exercise involved a workplace incident in which an employee was accused of cyberbullying staff and students through social media, company email and messaging applications.

The task focused on preparing for the **acquisition stage** and documenting how evidence would be secured, preserved and handed over for later forensic examination.

## Investigation Preparation

The original report identified several controls needed before attending the scene:

- evidence log sheet
- contemporary notes
- security-sealed evidence bags
- a forensic workstation / laptop
- clean storage media
- a write blocker
- FTK Imager
- digital camera
- tools for accessing storage devices where necessary

## Evidence Integrity

A major theme of the case study was preserving the original evidence.

### Chain of custody

The evidence log was intended to record what was seized, who handled it and when it changed hands.

### Contemporary notes

Actions, reasons and timestamps were to be recorded so that another investigator could understand and reproduce the process.

### Write protection

The report proposed connecting original storage media through a **write blocker** before forensic imaging to minimise the risk of modifying source evidence.

### Forensic imaging

FTK Imager was identified as the tool for creating a bit-for-bit copy of storage media where imaging was required.

## Scene Handling

The documented workflow included:

1. secure the scene
2. identify hazards and authorised personnel
3. begin contemporaneous documentation
4. identify victims and relevant evidence sources
5. photograph device state and physical layout
6. seize relevant devices
7. place devices in separate security-sealed bags
8. update the evidence log
9. hand evidence and documentation to the forensic examiner

## Analyst-Relevant Lessons

This exercise translates well to incident-response work because the same principle applies: **collect evidence carefully before changing the environment**.

For a SOC analyst, that can mean preserving logs, recording timestamps, documenting commands or containment actions, and ensuring that later responders can understand exactly what happened before and during the investigation.

## Limitation

This was an academic first-response scenario. The repository does not claim that I performed a real-world seizure or production forensic acquisition.
