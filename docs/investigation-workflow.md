# Investigation Workflow

## Practical Investigation Model

The two university case studies can be reduced to a repeatable analyst-style workflow.

### 1. Receive and Scope

Define:

- what is alleged
- when it happened
- who or what systems may be involved
- what evidence is already available

### 2. Form an Initial Hypothesis

Create a testable explanation rather than assuming the allegation is correct.

Example from the BYOD case:

> Did the identified mobile devices use the local network to access the relevant social-media service at the reported incident times?

### 3. Identify Evidence Sources

Potential sources in the coursework included:

- victim screenshots
- device state
- forensic images
- network communication records
- user / device ownership information
- policy documentation
- contemporary investigator notes

### 4. Preserve Before Changing

Before examination or containment:

- document the current state
- preserve relevant logs or evidence
- use write protection where forensic imaging is required
- record who handled the evidence

### 5. Correlate

Use common identifiers such as:

- timestamps
- source / destination
- device identity
- user identity
- application or service

### 6. Test the Hypothesis

Ask whether the available evidence:

- supports the hypothesis
- contradicts it
- or is insufficient

### 7. Escalate Where Necessary

If further access, authority or specialist examination is required, document the reason and escalate.

### 8. Record Findings and Handover

The final investigation record should allow another analyst or examiner to understand:

- what was known
- what was checked
- what was found
- what actions were taken
- what remains uncertain
